Thesis

One core, many markets

The expensive part of software is rarely the product. It's the plumbing you rebuild every time.

Ingestion, storage, auth, multi-tenancy, a data-verification loop, and a repeatable path to production — every serious product needs these, and most teams rebuild them from scratch per project. Aeglos treats that plumbing as a shared engine and puts the product-specific work on top of it. The result is that one builder can ship a defense-grade intelligence console and a public consumer marketplace from the same foundation, in the same quarter.

This paper covers nine products spanning intelligence, micromobility, B2B commerce, automotive data, operating systems, and on-device AI. They look unrelated. They aren't: each is the same engine, pointed at a different industry's unsolved problem.

01
SaaS Infrastructure · White-label platforms

The Aeglos Platform & Engine

The shared foundation every other product is built on.

The industry

Vertical SaaS and agencies live and die on time-to-launch. The market is full of teams that can design a great product for one client but can't reproduce it for the next without another full build — auth, billing, tenant isolation, deployment, and access control get re-implemented every engagement.

What it does

Aeglos-Platform and Aeglos-Engine provide the common layer: multi-tenant client portals, per-tenant deployments and feature flags, authentication, and a scripted deploy pipeline that ships behind a strict content-security policy. New products plug into this rather than reinventing it.

Why it matters

It collapses the cost of productization. A branded, client-ready data product goes from a multi-month rebuild to a configuration exercise. That is the entire reason the six products below could exist at once, built by one person.

Best suited for

  • Agencies productizing repeat client work
  • Vertical SaaS serving many tenants
  • Founders validating several bets cheaply
  • Internal platform teams standardizing delivery

Intelligence · OSINT / GEOINT

AEGLOS Analytics Pro

Fused, forecasted, correlated open-source intelligence on a single operator screen.

The industry

Serious intelligence tooling — fusion of many sources, geospatial correlation, forecasting — has historically been the domain of seven-figure government and enterprise platforms. Everyone else (corporate security, risk teams, journalists, NGOs, analysts) is left stitching together tabs, RSS, and spreadsheets.

What it does

The GeoThreat engine ingests open sources in real time, applies ML threat classification, clusters raw events into multi-source stories, runs 72-hour Prophet forecasting, and computes region-to-region correlation and AI convergence — surfaced in a Situation Room that ships as a one-click macOS app or a Docker stack.

Why it matters

It compresses a capability normally gated behind enormous contracts into something a single analyst can run locally. The value isn't more data — it's fused data: severity, geography, and trend on one screen, updating live.

In one line

Palantir-shaped capability, laptop-shaped footprint.

03
Micromobility · Consumer research & compliance

Aeglos eBike Intelligence

A trust layer for a market drowning in spec inflation and legal ambiguity.

The industry

Electric bikes and light electric vehicles are one of the fastest-growing consumer categories — and one of the noisiest. Marketing specs are routinely inflated, and the legal picture (Class 1/2/3 rules, power limits, helmet and age laws) fractures state by state. Buyers and dealers make expensive decisions on bad information.

What it does

aeglos.systems is a verified comparison engine across dozens of models, driven by a no-guess data pipeline — scrape, verify against official sources, then apply — so every spec is traceable. An interactive US eBike-law map turns the regulatory patchwork into something a normal person can read before they buy.

Why it matters

In a category defined by hype, verified data and legible law are the differentiators. It shifts the buyer from "which ad do I believe" to "which machine is actually legal and actually performs where I ride."

Best suited for

  • Consumers comparing before a major purchase
  • Dealers & resellers needing honest specs
  • Fleet buyers weighing compliance

05
Systems · Service continuity

Aeglos Assurance

Two modules for the two ways a service fails — going down, and getting cut off.

The industry

Any business that delivers an ongoing service — broadband, utilities, energy, SaaS, managed services — lives with two operational realities: the service can suffer an outage, and customers can be disconnected or terminated. Most teams track these in a scatter of spreadsheets, chat threads, and email, with no shared, current picture.

What it does

Assurance is a two-module suite. Beacon is a live status and incident board — post an outage, its scope and affected customers, attach evidence, and every operator sees it instantly. Register is a filterable record of disconnects and terminations with lookup and XLSX export. Both ingest from generic sources — HubSpot, SQL, CSV/XLSX — so the same tooling drops into any vertical without a rebuild.

Why it matters

It unifies the two failure modes into one operational picture, and it's source-agnostic by design — the difference between a telecom tool and a utility tool becomes a data connector, not a new product.

Under the hood

In production, Beacon and Register are being rewritten to back onto a server-managed SQLite datastore — a single, durable source of truth shared across the whole team rather than per-browser storage. Incidents, disconnects, and attached evidence live server-side and reconcile to every operator in real time, with source data ingested from HubSpot, SQL, or flat exports. The interactive demos here run entirely client-side as a standalone preview of the same interface.

Best suited for

  • Broadband & telecom support ops
  • Utilities & energy providers
  • SaaS & MSP operations teams
  • Subscription businesses tracking churn

06
Systems · Data distribution

DocSanctum

One canonical record, published top-down to every system that needs it.

The industry

Organizations that run on many systems — a knowledge base, a HubSpot, a custom knowledge management system, per-partner portals — maintain the same information in all of them. Every update means editing the same thing in several places, which guarantees drift, stale copies, and half-finished changes that users see.

What it does

DocSanctum makes one canonical record the single source of truth. Author or ingest it once — from email, meetings, or a form — and the console transforms it into the format each downstream system expects and fans it out in a single signed publish. Failed targets queue for retry with backoff; every change is logged by who, when, and why; and tenants are isolated by namespace.

Why it matters

It removes duplicate maintenance and the drift that comes with it. One change updates everywhere, atomically — end users never catch a half-published state — and the full audit trail makes it safe for regulated and partner-facing content.

Best suited for

  • Knowledge & support organizations
  • Franchise / multi-location operations
  • Compliance & policy distribution
  • White-label / reseller content

Automotive · Data & knowledge products

Car-Flipping Guide

Tribal resale knowledge, turned into a structured per-vehicle report.

The industry

Used-car arbitrage runs on scattered, tribal knowledge — which model years hide expensive faults, what a car really resells for, where demand is strongest. That intelligence lives in forums, private spreadsheets, and experience, not in any single usable product.

What it does

The Car-Flipping Guide is a data-first backend, not a marketplace. A full make/model/year catalog back to 1991, known-issues intelligence, monthly market-price snapshots fed by a dedicated scraper, and regional demand guidance — assembled into a single per-vehicle Flip Report.

Why it matters

It productizes judgment. Instead of years of accumulated gut feel, a flipper gets a structured buy/sell picture per vehicle: what to inspect, what it's worth, and where to move it.

08
Systems · Kiosk & appliance computing

Altimit OS

A web interface that is the operating system — bootable to real hardware.

The industry

Kiosks, digital signage, appliances, and single-purpose devices are usually built one of two painful ways: a locked-down native app that's slow to change, or a browser bolted onto a stock OS with no real system access. Neither is fast to build and genuinely capable.

What it does

Altimit OS makes the browser UI the shell of a real, multi-user Debian live distribution. A Python agent gives the sandboxed kiosk genuine host powers — filesystem, mail, networking — so a web-tech interface drives an actual machine. It ships as a hybrid BIOS/UEFI USB ISO that boots straight to the desktop.

Why it matters

It's a model for appliance and edge computing: build the entire experience in fast, themeable web tech, but keep real system capability underneath. Fast to iterate, trivial to brand, deployable to bare metal.

Best suited for

  • Kiosks & digital signage
  • Branded appliance experiences
  • Single-purpose & edge devices
  • Rapid OS/UX prototyping

R&D · On-device AI

Open-LLM VTuber

A conversational AI avatar that runs entirely on the device — no cloud.

The industry

Most conversational AI is a round trip to someone else's servers: cost per token, network latency, and every word leaving the device. For privacy-sensitive, offline, or regulated settings, that's a non-starter.

What it does

Open-LLM VTuber is a fully on-device pipeline — a local language model paired with voice synthesis and a live animated avatar, running in a real-time loop with no cloud dependency.

Why it matters

It's a working proof that capable conversational agents can live entirely at the edge — private by construction, zero marginal cost, and available offline. That's directly relevant to assistants in regulated industries, accessibility tools, and anything that can't send data out.

Best suited for

  • Privacy-first assistants
  • Offline / edge AI deployments
  • Streamers and interactive avatars
  • Regulated environments
